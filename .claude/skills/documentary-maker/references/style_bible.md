# STYLE BIBLE — "The Berkshires" Documentary Style
Derived from frame-by-frame + full-transcript analysis of the reference video
("How America's Millionaires Built a Paradise — Then Walked Away: The Berkshires", 36:21).

## 1. STRUCTURE (mandatory)
1. **Cold-open hook (60–90s):** A staggering verified statistic + a then-vs-now mystery.
   Reference: "By 1899, Lenox, Massachusetts, held more concentrated private fortune… than any
   town in America outside Newport… fewer than half of those 70-plus cottages are still standing."
   End the hook with the thesis: "This is the story of how…"
2. **Chapters (6–8):** chronological. Each chapter = one era/one turn of the story.
   Chapter titles are short and evocative: "The Valley Before the Money", "The Crescendo",
   "The Last Guest", "The Pivot", "What the Walls Forgot", "What Remains".
3. **Chekhov's Gun:** plant one concrete detail mid-video with the literal words
   "Keep that in mind." (reference: Carnegie's sprinkler system) → pay it off in the climax
   chapter with devastating irony.
4. **Every chapter ends on a cliffhanger** — a name, a date, or a threat:
   "His name is Carnegie." / "It will not stay that way." / "Not for another twenty-six years?"
5. **Final chapter = legacy/redemption**, ending on a poetic 2-word closer: "Stone. Quiet."

## 2. SCRIPT VOICE (mandatory formulas, with reference examples)
- **Slow prestige pace:** ~140 spoken words/minute. Short paragraphs. Room to breathe.
- **Punch fragments:** "Some burned. Some were simply emptied out, sold, and forgotten."
- **Money as a character with will:** "the money caught up to it" / "that wealth walked away."
- **Arithmetic as metaphor:** "Three houses, three separate arithmetics, in the same six-mile valley."
- **Inflation anchoring:** always convert historical sums: "$3,000,000 in 1890, a sum worth
  something in the neighborhood of $85,000,000 today."
- **Brutal compression:** state a tragedy as pure math: "A woman built a $950,000 house from a
  fortune of $3,000,000 and used it for three summers."
- **Sensory scene-painting** once per major chapter: "The air carries cut grass and horse
  leather, and beneath both, wood smoke from a hundred kitchens working at once."
- **Repetition for weight:** "Vanderbilt money paid for it, Morgan money paid for it,
  Carnegie money paid for it."
- **Understated irony at payoff:** "And it does not fire a single valve. Not because it fails.
  Because nobody in the building that night knows it is there."
- NO first person, NO "welcome to the channel", NO questions to the viewer except rhetorical.

## 3. VISUALS (mandatory)
- **REAL archival photos ONLY** — B&W / sepia period photographs, postcards, engravings,
  paintings-of-record, real modern photos for the "today" sections. **NEVER AI-generated.**
- ~80% of runtime = treated archival stills with Ken Burns motion.
- **Treatment:** historical scenes → desaturate to B&W or gentle sepia, +contrast, soft vignette.
  "Today/legacy" scenes → keep color (the ONLY color in the film = emotional release at the end).
- **Motion:** every still moves — slow zoom in/out or pan; alternate directions between scenes.
  Zoom range 1.0→1.12 max. NEVER static, NEVER fast.
- **NO HARD CUTS.** Every transition = 1s crossfade (or fade-through-black at chapter breaks).
- **Title cards:** white serif (Playfair/Goudy-class) centered on pure black. Chapter number in
  small caps above the title.
- **Stat cards:** one huge number or 2–4 word phrase in big serif, overlaid or on black
  (reference: "EIGHTY ROOMS" over the mansion photo).
- **Map cards:** parchment/cream background, serif labels, a red location pin, dotted route line.
- Effective shot length 8–30s. Average ~15–25s.

## 4. AUDIO (mandatory)
- **Narration:** deep, somber, authoritative male voice; slow and deliberate; melancholic gravitas.
  Mix at ~-16 LUFS, peaks ≤ -3dB.
- **Music beds:** minor-key orchestral strings (main body), somber solo piano (tragedy/fire
  chapters), warm resolving piano/strings (legacy chapter). Music sits ~-20 to -24dB under voice.
- **Silence is allowed** — after big payoff lines, let 1–2s of near-silence sit.
- Optional subtle foley (hooves, fire crackle) at ≤ -26dB. Never distracting.

## 5. QUALITY BARS
- Every fact in the script must be real and sourced (research_notes.md).
- Every image must be a real photograph/artwork of (or period-accurate to) its subject.
- Sync: each scene's image is on screen for exactly its narration + ~0.8s.
- Output: 1920x1080, 30fps, H.264 yuv420p, AAC 44.1kHz stereo.
